TL;DR

  • Knowledge transfer: general engineering principles, not a tour of any codebase or product.
  • Three sources: published practice, private study notes, and lessons from real systems.
  • Every principle says where it came from: Practice, Notes, Lesson, or Case.
  • Twelve docs, one per theme. Each section ends with an exercise and a check.
  • Priorities: P1 core for everyone, P2 depth for owners, P3 reference.

What this set is and is not

Principles that hold across stacks, each with its reason and its source.

It is

  • General principles with their why
  • Sourced and labelled
  • Written for engineers joining the team

It is not

  • Onboarding onto one codebase
  • A product tour
  • The team's internal standards

Examples are generic. A lesson from a real system is stated as a rule, without naming the system.

Source labels

Every principle says where it came from, so you know how to check it.

Practice

Standards, OWASP, the SRE books, vendor and framework docs. A link, checked on 2026-10-26.

Notes

Private study notes: cloud architecture, a platform review, a GenAI field guide, Python. No ref.

Lesson

Learned building or reviewing real systems. Stated as a rule; the system is not named.

Case

The hypothetical worked case in track J.

Evidence badges and doc tags

A number is measured, derived or a target. Say which before you quote it.

Measured
It came out of a run.
Derived
Read from versioned config: what is declared, not always what runs.
Target
Intended, with nothing behind it yet. Never present it as achieved.
Page badges
inferred unverified proposal. No badge means seen in the source.

How to run a learning session

Five steps per section, done in pairs.

  1. Read the principle Aloud, 5 minutes.
  2. Open one Practice link Find the sentence it rests on, 10 minutes.
  3. Do the exercise In pairs, 30 minutes.
  4. Answer the check Without notes.
  5. Apply it One sentence on where it fits your service.

Verify before sharing

A few facts are dated or disputed. Check them before you quote them.

One source disagrees with AWS

The platform review says 50% of one task "rounds down to none". ECS documents rounding the minimum up. This set follows the documentation.

  • Cloud product names, checked September 2026, and EU AI Act and Data Act dates.
  • The platform review is anonymised and undated. Date any number before quoting it.
  • The case deck's company ticks were confirmed by a team, not read from config.
  • Public pages move. The links were checked on 2026-10-26.
